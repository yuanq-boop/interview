# Optimizing LLM-as-a-Judge / Agent-as-a-Judge Evaluators Against Human Ratings (2023–2026), with Emphasis on Agentic and Multimodal Judges of Web Apps, Visuals, and Interactive/3D Content

WebFetch to arxiv.org, huggingface.co and hamel.dev was blocked by the session's network allowlist, so most findings come from search-result extracts of primary pages (arXiv HTML/PDF, ACL Anthology, CVPR open access, ICLR, MLflow docs) plus some secondary summaries. ## 1. Agreement metrics, calibration, and known judge biases (and mitigations)

### Takeaway
Raw percent agreement is the most common metric, and it overstates alignment. Use chance-corrected agreement (Cohen's/weighted kappa or Krippendorff's alpha), per-class rates (TPR/TNR), and rank correlation (Kendall/Spearman) for system-level rankings, and always compare against the human-human ceiling. Well-documented biases are position, verbosity/length, self-preference, and high-score/leniency bias; in multimodal judges add compositional/grounding bias. Pairwise comparison with order-swapping is consistently more reliable than absolute scoring.

### Cited Findings
- **Human ceiling (2023-06):** Zheng et al. "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (NeurIPS 2023 D&B) report that GPT-4 judges reach over 80% agreement with controlled and crowdsourced human preferences, about the same as human-human agreement. — [NeurIPS poster](https://nips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/73434); [arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05685). Secondary sources give 85% GPT-4-vs-human against 81% human-human (secondary, unverified) — [Adaline blog](https://www.adaline.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-reliability-bias)
- **Self-preference (2023):** In MT-Bench, GPT-4 gave its own answers about a 10% win-rate boost and Claude-v1 about 25%, while GPT-3.5 showed none, so the effect depends on the model (secondary, unverified). One explanation offered is perplexity: models prefer text that reads like their own. — [Grepture blog](https://grepture.com/blog/llm-as-a-judge-bias); [memx blog](https://memx.app/blog/llm-as-a-judge-biases-and-fixes/)
- **Position consistency:** Reportedly even the strongest MT-Bench judge gave the same verdict only 65.0% of the time when the two answers were swapped (secondary, unverified). — [memx blog](https://memx.app/blog/llm-as-a-judge-biases-and-fixes/)
- **Percent agreement vs kappa (2024-06):** Thakur et al. "Judging the Judges: Evaluating Alignment and Vulnerabilities in LLMs-as-Judges" show that judges with high percent agreement can still give very different scores. In their comparison, percent agreement showed a 30-point gap between human judgment and exact match, while Cohen's kappa showed a 53-point gap. They recommend kappa plus qualitative analysis, warning that even kappa "is not all telling". — [arXiv 2406.12624](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12624v1)
- Example: a judge that always outputs "tie" on a mostly-tie dataset gets high raw agreement but kappa near zero. — [Kili Technology blog](https://kili-technology.com/blog/data-story-who-judges-the-judge-llm-as-a-judge)
- **Position bias (2025):** "A Systematic Study of Position Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" (IJCNLP-AACL 2025) measures position bias across judges and tasks. One variant reports the second position systematically favored (coefficient −0.165). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/402590616_Judging_the_Judges_A_Systematic_Study_of_Position_Bias_in_LLM-as-a-Judge)
- **Bias metric definitions (2026-04):** "Judging the Judges: A Systematic Evaluation of Bias Mitigation Strategies in LLM-as-a-Judge Pipelines" reports human agreement plus Cohen's kappa and defines signed bias scores for position, verbosity (does the judge favor the longer response), style, and self-preference (does it favor its own model family). — [arXiv 2604.23178](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23178)
- **Family-conditioned preference in panels (2026-09):** "Who Judges Matters" measures how a judge's model family shifts preferences in LLM-judge panels. This is relevant to ensembles and juries. — [arXiv 2609.17857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17857)
- **Juries:** Practitioners report that panels of weaker judges from different model families can outperform a single strong judge. — [orq.ai blog](https://orq.ai/blog/llm-juries-in-practice)
- **Multimodal judges (2024-02):** MLLM-as-a-Judge (Chen et al., ICML 2024) found GPT-4V reached about 78% human agreement on pairwise comparison (Gemini about 72%) and about 70% averaged across settings. Absolute scoring and batch ranking were much worse, with "significant bias, hallucination, and inconsistency". Some models showed **high-score bias** (mostly 4–5 ratings). LLaVA and CogVLM showed strong position bias. GPT-4V's egocentric (self-preference) bias was slight. — [arXiv 2402.04788](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04788); [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.04788); [Emergent Mind topic summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/mllm-as-a-judge-mechanism)
- **Compositional bias in MLLM judges (2026-04):** MM-JudgeBias defines "compositional bias", where the judge does not reliably ground its verdict in the full multimodal context, and benchmarks 9 bias types. — [arXiv 2604.18164](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18164v3)
- Vision-Judge style follow-ups report MLLM judges remain biased toward verbose or high-confidence candidates on vision-centric tasks. — [Emergent Mind topic summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/mllm-as-a-judge-mechanism)
- **Leniency in rubric judges (2026-07):** CalibratedRubric reports LLM judges label positives more often than human gold. Filtering rubric items for measurability raised human-gold agreement on JudgmentBench from κ=0.604 to κ=0.743. — [arXiv 2607.29252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29252)
- **Per-class rates:** Practitioner guidance from Hamel Husain says raw agreement misleads under class imbalance, so report TPR and TNR separately. A judge that rubber-stamps PASS can show about 90% agreement while catching no failures (secondary descriptions of Husain's guide). — [hypeflo.ws summary](https://www.hypeflo.ws/workflow/building-llm-as-a-judge-for-ai-product-evaluation-using-critique-shadowing); [schristoph blog](https://schristoph.online/blog/your-ai-judge-needs-a-judge/?utm=series); [hamelsmu/evals-skills](https://github.com/hamelsmu/evals-skills/blob/main/questions.md)
- **Deployment threshold heuristic:** One practitioner suggests trusting a judge as a standalone evaluator only at Krippendorff's alpha ≥ 0.80 with humans, and otherwise using it only as a first-pass filter (informal blog guidance, not a standard). — [knomit blog](https://knomit.io/blog/unattended-llm-judges/)
- **Rank-level metrics for system leaderboards:** GPTEval3D uses Kendall's tau between metric-induced and expert model rankings. ArtifactsBench uses normalized Footrule ranking consistency against WebDev Arena (details in §4). — [CVPR 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wu_GPT-4Vision_is_a_Human-Aligned_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); [arXiv 2507.04952](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04952)
- **Annotation protocol raises the ceiling:** WebDevJudge's rubric-guided human protocol reached over 80% inter-annotator agreement, compared with 63% for MT-Bench. — [arXiv 2510.18560v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v2); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010879)
- **Pairwise beats pointwise for web-app judging:** On WebDevJudge, pairwise comparison beat single-answer scoring by an average of more than 8 points of agreement across models. Likert or rubric guidance added only marginal gains in the pairwise setting (secondary summary). — [Liner KR review](https://liner.com/ko/review/webdevjudge-evaluating-mllms-as-critiques-for-web-development-quality)

### Inferences
- For a small human-rated set (hundreds of items) with rubric scores on ordinal scales, the right primary metrics are quadratic-weighted kappa or Krippendorff's alpha (ordinal) per dimension, plus Spearman/Kendall for ranking. Report each against the human-human ceiling, for example as judge-human kappa divided by human-human kappa. Raw agreement alone will overstate alignment, especially with leniency or high-score bias.
- Pairwise-with-swap (score both orders, count only consistent verdicts or average them) is the most robust judging format for visual and web artifacts. Absolute scores tend to cluster high in MLLMs.
- Self-preference matters when the judge model family is the same as the generator family, a common setup in self-improving harnesses. Use cross-family judges or juries.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the primary text for MT-Bench self-preference percentages or the 65% swap-consistency figure; both are from secondary blogs.
- I found no primary study that measures "halo effects across rubric dimensions" (inter-dimension score correlation inflated relative to humans) specifically for multimodal or web-app judges. This is a known concern, but no citation was retrieved in this session.
- Panickssery et al. (2024) on self-recognition causing self-preference was not retrieved.
- No source here compares quadratic-weighted kappa with Spearman for judge validation directly.

## 2. Automated optimization of judge prompts and rubrics using human labels (EvalGen, criteria drift, DSPy/GEPA/MIPRO, AutoCalibrate, LLM-Rubric, Prometheus, fine-tuning vs prompting, rationales as signal)

### Takeaway
The dominant 2024–2026 pattern treats the judge prompt or rubric as a learnable artifact. You collect human labels (ideally with free-text rationales), measure disagreement, and have an LLM optimizer (GEPA/MIPRO/COPRO, AutoCalibrate-style criteria refinement, or simple disagreement-driven rewrites) revise the instructions and few-shot examples. Human criteria themselves drift during labeling, so the loop must allow rubric revision, not just judge fitting. Lightweight calibration on top of LLM outputs (LLM-Rubric) and measurability filtering of rubric items (CalibratedRubric) are cheap, effective complements. Fine-tuned open judges (Prometheus 2) do not reliably beat strong prompted or optimized frontier judges.

### Cited Findings
- **EvalGen / criteria drift (UIST 2024; arXiv 2024-04):** Shankar, Zamfirescu-Pereira, Hartmann, Parameswaran, and Arawjo, "Who Validates the Validators?". EvalGen proposes candidate criteria and implementations (code assertions or LLM graders). Users grade a subset of outputs, and those grades select the implementations that best match the user. Main finding: **criteria drift**. "Users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria". Participants kept refining criteria and even went back to change earlier grades, so it is "impossible to completely determine evaluation criteria prior to human judging of LLM outputs". — [Berkeley PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bjoern/papers/shankar-validators-uist2024.pdf); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10646607). The study was a qualitative user study (n≈9 per secondary summaries, unverified). — [Arize breakdown](https://arize.com/blog/breaking-down-evalgen-who-validates-the-validators/)
- **DSPy teleprompters for judges (2024-12):** "A Comparative Study of DSPy Teleprompter Algorithms for Aligning LLM Evaluation Metrics to Human Evaluation" optimizes a hallucination-detection judge prompt against human labels with COPRO, MIPRO, BootstrapFewShot (with random search), and others. It frames the judge prompt as the object to optimize. — [arXiv 2412.15298](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15298v1)
- **GEPA judge alignment in production tooling (2025–2026):** MLflow ships a GEPA alignment optimizer built on DSPy's implementation. It reflects on execution traces and proposes revised judge instructions from human feedback. Requirements: at least 10 human-labeled traces using the same name as the judge, ideally a mix of positive and negative labels, and **natural-language rationales "highly recommended"**. GEPA is recommended for judges with nuanced criteria. MLflow's alignment page also offers MemAlign (the default, memory/few-shot based) and SIMBA. — [MLflow GEPA docs](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/genai/eval-monitor/scorers/llm-judge/gepa/); [MLflow judge alignment](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/genai/eval-monitor/scorers/llm-judge/alignment/); [gepa-ai/gepa GitHub](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa)
- **Rationales as optimizer feedback (practitioner, 2025):** Explosion's Prodigy + DSPy workflow wraps the judge metric so GEPA receives the human's written feedback, and reports about a 26% improvement in its human-aligned judge metric after optimization. — [Explosion blog](https://explosion.ai/blog/human-aligned-llm-evaluation-dspy)
- **Disagreement-driven rewrite loop (practitioner):** Humans label independently instead of correcting the judge, to avoid anchoring. An LLM proposes a rewritten judge prompt from the disagreements, and the new prompt is re-run on the flipped cases to confirm. — [orq.ai blog](https://orq.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-alignment-with-humans)
- **Small-data result (2026-08):** A drug-discovery agent-eval paper used DSPy LabeledFewShot on 30 annotated samples and raised alignment from 0.80 to 0.86 on validation (very small sample). — [arXiv 2608.21057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21057)
- **AutoCalibrate (2023-09, Liu et al.):** A gradient-free, multi-stage method. An LLM drafts scoring criteria from a few human-labeled examples, then is shown instances where its scores **disagreed with experts** and asked to refine the criteria. It reports improved human correlation on summarization and hallucination-detection tasks (no figures retrieved). — [Lacuna project page](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/calibrating-llm-based-evaluator/wrk_193977caaf436bfebf821b230e484505)
- **LLM-Rubric (ACL 2024; Hashemi et al.):** Uses a manually written multi-question rubric. The LLM's probability distribution over each rubric answer feeds a small **calibration network trained on human annotations** that models each individual judge (judge-specific and judge-independent parameters). It predicts human overall-satisfaction scores within about 0.4 on a 1–4 scale, roughly 2× better than the uncalibrated LLM (secondary summary). — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.745v1.pdf); [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2501.00274)
- **Prometheus 2 (2024-05):** An open evaluator LM that takes instruction, reference answer, and score rubric, for direct assessment and pairwise ranking. Later papers report that Prometheus 2 (8×7B) **underperforms the best training-free methods** despite rubric-specific training. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386192699_Prometheus_2_An_Open_Source_Language_Model_Specialized_in_Evaluating_Other_Language_Models); [arXiv 2508.12792 "Bridging Human and LLM Judgments"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.12792)
- **Regression probing as a calibration alternative (2026-01):** BoRP (Bootstrapped Regression Probing) trains probes for scalable, human-aligned scoring, an alternative to prompting or fine-tuning judges. — [arXiv 2601.18253](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18253)
- **Rubric banks and measurability filtering (2026-07):** CalibratedRubric builds task-adaptive rubric banks. Dropping rubric items that are not reliably measurable raised κ from 0.604 to 0.743 with human gold. — [arXiv 2607.29252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29252)
- **Dynamic rubric generation/refinement:** "Generating and Refining Dynamic Evaluation Rubrics for LLM-as-a-Judge" (2026) is part of the rubric-refinement line. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405562105_Generating_and_Refining_Dynamic_Evaluation_Rubrics_for_LLM-as-a-Judge)
- **Critique shadowing (Hamel Husain, 2024-10):** A domain expert gives binary pass/fail plus a short written critique for each item. The critiques become few-shot examples in the judge prompt, and you iterate until the judge agrees with the expert. Husain recommends binary labels over Likert, error analysis before metrics, and code-based checks before LLM judges. In the Honeycomb case, reported agreement exceeded 90% with the expert after about 3 iterations on a roughly balanced set (secondary summaries; primary hamel.dev fetch blocked). He says the process "never truly ends" and should be repeated when inputs or the product change. — [Latent Space / AINews](https://latent.space/p/ainews-legacy-24-10-30-ainews-creating-a-llm-as-a-judge); [hypeflo.ws summary](https://www.hypeflo.ws/workflow/building-llm-as-a-judge-for-ai-product-evaluation-using-critique-shadowing); [hamelsmu/evals-skills](https://github.com/hamelsmu/evals-skills/blob/main/questions.md)
- **Human corrections as calibration data:** LangChain/LangSmith describes storing human corrections to judge outputs as few-shot examples (a self-improving evaluator). — [LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/resources/llm-as-a-judge)

### Inferences
- A practical loop for a critic agent with human rubric scores and rationales: (1) split the human set into train/dev/test, stratified by dimension and score. (2) Run the baseline judge and compute per-dimension weighted kappa and TPR/TNR. (3) Cluster disagreements and mine the human rationales for missing criteria. Feed the rationale text to a reflective optimizer (GEPA/MIPRO, or an AutoCalibrate-style "here are cases where you disagreed with experts, revise the criteria" step). (4) Accept a new rubric or prompt only if it improves held-out agreement (as ReLook's "forced optimization" accepts only improving revisions; see §4). (5) Periodically re-label to catch criteria drift.
- Rationales matter most because they carry the *why*. GEPA-style reflective optimizers and MLflow explicitly use them, while pure scalar labels only support few-shot selection or calibration.
- With hundreds of items, prompt/rubric optimization plus a light calibration layer (LLM-Rubric-style per-dimension mapping from judge logits to human scale) is more data-efficient than fine-tuning a judge. The Prometheus 2 evidence suggests fine-tuned judges do not dominate.
- Overfitting risk: with fewer than 100 labels per dimension, optimizer gains (for example 0.80→0.86 on 30 samples) are within noise. Use a held-out split and bootstrap CIs.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve head-to-head numbers comparing GEPA vs MIPROv2 vs few-shot for *judge* alignment specifically. The GEPA paper's general prompt-optimization results were not retrieved.
- I did not retrieve quantitative AutoCalibrate results. The Lacuna page gives none.
- I found no direct study that uses human *free-text rationales* as a contrastive training signal for a *multimodal/web* judge (as opposed to text judges).
- No controlled fine-tune-vs-prompt comparison for multimodal judges was retrieved.

## 3. Agent-as-a-Judge and later tool-using agentic judges; does tool use improve agreement?

### Takeaway
Tool use helps when the evidence must be gathered from the artifact itself, such as reading a codebase, running code, or navigating an app. Agent-as-a-Judge (2024) is the flagship positive result (90% vs 70% agreement with human consensus). The evidence is mixed for GUI-driving judges on web apps, where compounding agent errors can make agentic judges *worse* than direct MLLM judges (WebDevJudge, 2025). Agentic judges also tend to confirm passes better than they catch failures.

### Cited Findings
- **Agent-as-a-Judge (Zhuge et al., arXiv 2024-10; ICML 2025):** Evaluated on DevAI, a benchmark of 55 realistic AI-dev tasks with hierarchical requirements. An agentic judge (built on OpenHands-style tooling that reads files, locates evidence, and checks requirements) matched the human-expert consensus in **90%** of cases versus **70%** for LLM-as-a-Judge. It was also far cheaper and faster than human evaluation. — [arXiv 2410.10934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934); [ICML 2025 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45485)
- **Auto-Eval Judge (2025-08):** A general agentic task-completion judge framework that reports 4.76% and 10.52% higher human alignment over a baseline LLM judge on its benchmarks (snippet-level; exact settings unverified). — [arXiv 2508.05508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05508)
- **Survey (2025-08):** "When AIs Judge AIs: The Rise of Agent-as-a-Judge Evaluation for LLMs" surveys the shift from static LLM judges to agentic judges with planning, tools, and memory. — [arXiv 2508.02994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02994)
- **Online Agent-as-a-Judge (2026-06):** A situation-generating judge for interactive agents correctly identified 93/114 (82%) human-labeled passes but only 87/144 (60%) human-labeled failures. **Agentic judges are weaker at catching failures than confirming passes.** — [arXiv 2606.08200](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08200v1)
- **AgentJudgeBench (2026-08):** A multi-difficulty benchmark for evaluating LLM judges on agentic tool-calling trajectories. — [arXiv 2608.26623](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26623v1)
- **WebDevJudge agentic result (2025-10; ICLR 2026 oral):** The benchmark evaluates LLMs, MLLMs, and **agentic workflows** under static and interactive settings. Reportedly an agentic setup using the UI-TARS-1.5 GUI agent as executor *underperformed* vanilla models, attributed to compounding errors. Code-only LLM evaluators showed high recall but low precision on feasibility verification (secondary; primary tables not verified). — [arXiv 2510.18560v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v2); [ICLR 2026 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/oral/10010880); [Maxim blog](https://www.getmaxim.ai/blog/can-llms-actually-judge-web-development-quality-spoiler-not-really/); [Medium review](https://medium.com/@Micheal-Lanham/webdevjudge-and-the-limit-of-llm-judges-for-working-web-apps-58126411e939)
- **Practitioner guidance:** Use an agent judge when finding the relevant evidence is itself part of the evaluation, as in web-app testing where the judge must decide what to inspect. — [Arize guide](https://arize.com/?p=32829)
- **Agentic functional testers in web-gen benchmarks:** WebGen-Bench (2025) checks functionality with a navigation agent executing test cases and grades appearance with GPT-4o (1–5), with no blinded expert pairwise judgments. — [WebCoderBench comparison table, arXiv 2601.02430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02430); [Awesome-Code-Benchmark](https://github.com/tongye98/Awesome-Code-Benchmark)

### Inferences
- Tool use (code execution, DOM inspection, scripted interaction) most reliably improves *functional* and *requirement-satisfaction* judgments. For *aesthetic/visual* judgments, a well-prompted MLLM on screenshots may match or beat a GUI-driving agent, because each extra agent step adds error.
- A hybrid design looks best supported: deterministic scripted interaction (Playwright scripts generated from the task, screenshot or video capture at fixed states) for evidence, then an MLLM judge scoring the collected evidence. This is preferable to a free-roaming GUI agent that both explores and judges.
- Because agentic judges under-detect failures (60% vs 82%), track TNR separately and consider asymmetric thresholds.

### Gaps
- I found no published study that directly measures whether a Playwright-based agentic judge improves human agreement on generated web apps versus screenshot-only judging (the search explicitly turned up none).
- I could not verify WebDevJudge's exact agentic-vs-vanilla numbers from the primary paper (fetch blocked).

## 4. Judges for generated web apps, front-end, interactive and 3D content: agreement with humans and failure modes

### Takeaway
Benchmarks that capture *dynamic* evidence (multiple timed screenshots, interaction) and use fine-grained per-task checklists report high MLLM-judge agreement with humans: ArtifactsBench reports about 91% pairwise and 94.4% ranking consistency with WebDev Arena. Harder, more controlled meta-evaluations (WebDevJudge) show a large residual gap, with best judges around 70% vs about 85% human, on functional equivalence, feasibility, and bias. 3D evaluators (GPTEval3D) achieve good system-level rank correlation but are weaker on plausibility and geometry dimensions. Static screenshots miss interactivity and animation, which is why recent work captures temporal screenshot sequences.

### Cited Findings
- **ArtifactsBench (Tencent Hunyuan, arXiv 2025-07):** 1,825 tasks for visual-interactive code artifacts (benchmark size from background knowledge; confirm in the paper). Generated artifacts are rendered and their dynamic behavior captured through **temporal screenshots**. An MLLM-as-Judge then scores with a **fine-grained per-task checklist**. Reported results: up to **90.95% pairwise agreement with human experts**, and **94.4% ranking consistency with WebDev Arena** (normalized Footrule, Gemini-2.5-Pro judge). The judge was validated on a controlled human study (described as a 280-instance, 6-model study) before full deployment. — [arXiv 2507.04952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04952); [project page](https://artifactsbenchmark.github.io/); [GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/ArtifactsBenchmark). ReLook's citation of ArtifactsBench reports kappa > 0.85 and Spearman 0.87 (Gemini-2.5-Pro) and 0.83 (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) against crowd preferences over 200 tasks (secondary, unverified). — [ReLook arXiv 2510.11498](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11498)
- **WebDevJudge (Li et al., arXiv 2025-10; ICLR 2026 oral):** Meta-evaluates LLM, MLLM, and agentic judges on paired web implementations with human preference labels plus structured, query-grounded rubrics, under static (code/screenshot) and interactive (live environment) settings. Human inter-annotator agreement exceeded 80% (84.56% per one review) on 654 web apps, while the best LLM judges reach about 70% pairwise agreement. The gap is attributed to failures in **recognizing functional equivalence**, **verifying task feasibility**, and **mitigating bias**. — [arXiv 2510.18560v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v2); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010879); [Medium review (figures secondary)](https://medium.com/@Micheal-Lanham/webdevjudge-and-the-limit-of-llm-judges-for-working-web-apps-58126411e939); [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/li2026iclr-webdevjudge/)
- Note the tension: ArtifactsBench's about 91% and WebDevJudge's about 70% are not directly comparable. Differences include task distribution, how hard the paired items are, whether a per-task checklist was provided, and system-level vs item-level agreement. — [Search synthesis of both papers](https://medium.com/@Micheal-Lanham/webdevjudge-and-the-limit-of-llm-judges-for-working-web-apps-58126411e939)
- **ReLook (arXiv 2025-10; ACL 2026):** Uses an MLLM critic (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) as a tool and reward in RL for agentic web coding. The critic sees the prompt, the code, and a **series of temporal screenshots**. **Anti-reward-hacking rules:** zero reward if any required screenshot is invalid (render failure or timeout). A first attempt that penalized regressions led the agent to *reflect less* to avoid penalties, which is reward hacking. It was replaced by "Forced Optimization", which only accepts improving revisions. Largest gains (+3.2–6.1) came on visual-centric tasks (A-SVG, A-Game, A-Web). The critic is dropped at inference in favor of a critic-free self-edit loop. — [arXiv 2510.11498v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11498v1); [ACL 2026 PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1167.pdf); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=p2WKzAF3Za)
- **WebGen-Bench (Lu et al., 2025):** 101 instructions to generate full websites. Functionality is verified by a navigation agent running test cases, and appearance is graded by GPT-4o on a 1–5 scale. — [WebCoderBench table, arXiv 2601.02430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02430); [WebCraftBench, arXiv 2609.15387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15387)
- **WebCraftBench (2026-09):** Evaluates web-app generation from a software-testing perspective and reports 85.3% agreement with human preferences, with rankings stable across judge models. — [arXiv 2609.15387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15387)
- **Design2Code (2024-03; NAACL 2025):** 484 real webpages. It validates *automatic visual-similarity metrics* against human ratings rather than an MLLM judge. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392506178_Design2Code_Benchmarking_Multimodal_Code_Generation_for_Automated_Front-End_Engineering)
- **FullFront (arXiv 2025-05):** Covers the full front-end workflow (design, perception QA, code generation). Its human check was a blind evaluation on FullFront-mini using Gemini Visual Score criteria, which confirmed closed models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4.1) were rated more accurate (often >8/10). No judge-human agreement statistic was found. — [arXiv 2505.17399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17399)
- **UI-Bench (2025-08):** Evaluates design capabilities of text-to-app tools with expert pairwise comparisons. — [arXiv 2508.20410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20410)
- **Other multi-turn and feedback benchmarks:** EvoGenUI-Bench (2026-08, multi-turn generative UI) — [arXiv 2608.29387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29387). FronTalk (2026-01, conversational front-end with multimodal feedback) — [arXiv 2601.04203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04203)
- **GPTEval3D (Wu et al., CVPR 2024):** GPT-4V generates evaluation prompts, then compares pairs of 3D assets (multi-view renders) against user-defined criteria. Pairwise outcomes are turned into Elo ratings. Alignment is measured with Kendall's tau against expert model rankings. It reports the best correlation on 4 of 5 criteria, average τ≈0.710, text-asset alignment ≈0.821, and 3D plausibility ≈0.641 (figures from a secondary summary, unverified). The authors call it a proof of concept that depends on GPT-4V's capabilities and biases. — [CVPR 2024 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wu_GPT-4Vision_is_a_Human-Aligned_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); [arXiv 2401.04092](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04092v2); [Liner summary](https://liner.com/review/gpt4vision-is-humanaligned-evaluator-for-textto3d-generation)

### Inferences
- Failure modes that recur across these papers, and that a web, visual, or 3D critic should design against:
  1. **Static evidence misses dynamics.** A single screenshot cannot show animation, hover/click states, or game logic. ArtifactsBench and ReLook capture temporal screenshot sequences.
  2. **Functional-equivalence blindness.** Judges penalize implementations that differ from expectations but work.
  3. **Feasibility verification.** Judges cannot tell whether a feature actually works without running it. Code-reading judges are high-recall and low-precision.
  4. **Geometry and plausibility perception in 3D is weaker** than text-alignment perception.
  5. **Leniency and high-score bias** in absolute scoring.
- Per-task checklists (ArtifactsBench) are probably the biggest single lever for item-level agreement. They convert holistic visual judgment into verifiable sub-claims.
- When an MLLM critic is used as an RL reward, invalid-render guards and accept-only-improvements rules are needed to prevent hacking (ReLook).

### Gaps
- I found no paper that quantifies MLLM judges' ability to perceive *animation and motion quality* from video vs frame sequences for generated web content. Video-judge benchmarks were not retrieved.
- ArtifactsBench's exact human-study size (280 vs 200 instances) and the kappa/Spearman figures differ between sources and were not verified from the primary PDF.
- WebDev Arena's own methodology (human pairwise votes on live apps, Elo/Bradley-Terry) was not retrieved from a primary LMArena source.
- No retrieved 3D evaluator newer than GPTEval3D (for example 3DGen-Bench or Eval3D) with human-agreement numbers.
- WebArena-style programmatic verifiers vs LLM verifiers were not covered.

## 5. Handling noisy or disagreeing human labels, small datasets (hundreds of items), and per-dimension scoring

### Takeaway
Practitioners favor (a) binary or low-cardinality labels with written critiques over fine Likert scales, (b) independent labeling so humans don't anchor on the judge, (c) rubric protocols that raise inter-annotator agreement before judge fitting, (d) per-annotator calibration (LLM-Rubric) instead of averaging away disagreement, (e) dropping unmeasurable rubric items, and (f) small optimizer-friendly datasets (tens to low hundreds of examples) with held-out validation and periodic re-labeling for drift.

### Cited Findings
- **Binary over Likert, with critiques:** Husain recommends binary pass/fail plus a written critique, and starting with error analysis rather than metrics (secondary). — [hypeflo.ws summary](https://www.hypeflo.ws/workflow/building-llm-as-a-judge-for-ai-product-evaluation-using-critique-shadowing); [hamelsmu/evals-skills](https://github.com/hamelsmu/evals-skills/blob/main/questions.md)
- **Independent labeling to avoid anchoring:** Have humans label without seeing the judge's verdict, then optimize on the disagreements. — [orq.ai blog](https://orq.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-alignment-with-humans)
- **Rubric protocols raise human agreement:** WebDevJudge's structured, query-grounded rubric annotation reached over 80% inter-annotator agreement vs 63% for MT-Bench. — [arXiv 2510.18560v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v2)
- **Model each annotator rather than average:** LLM-Rubric's calibration network has judge-specific parameters, so it predicts individual human judges' scores and absorbs systematic annotator differences. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.745v1.pdf); [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2501.00274)
- **Drop unmeasurable rubric items:** In CalibratedRubric, measurability filtering raised κ from 0.604 to 0.743. — [arXiv 2607.29252](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.29252)
- **Small labeled sets suffice to start:** MLflow GEPA alignment needs at least 10 labeled traces, with a positive/negative mix and rationales. — [MLflow GEPA docs](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/genai/eval-monitor/scorers/llm-judge/gepa/). A DSPy run on 30 samples improved 0.80→0.86. — [arXiv 2608.21057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21057). ArtifactsBench validated its judge on a few hundred human-rated instances before scaling. — [project page](https://artifactsbenchmark.github.io/)
- **Labels are the bottleneck:** DSPy optimization reduces repeated expert review but still depends on labeled data. — [Explosion blog](https://explosion.ai/blog/human-aligned-llm-evaluation-dspy); [arXiv 2412.15298](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15298v1)
- **Criteria drift means labels go stale:** Users revise criteria and earlier grades as they see more outputs. — [Berkeley PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~bjoern/papers/shankar-validators-uist2024.pdf). Husain says alignment must be repeated periodically or when the product changes (secondary). — [schristoph blog](https://schristoph.online/blog/your-ai-judge-needs-a-judge/?utm=series)
- **Report chance-corrected and per-class metrics:** Use kappa over percent agreement — [arXiv 2406.12624](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12624v1). Use TPR/TNR separately under imbalance (secondary). — [hypeflo.ws](https://www.hypeflo.ws/workflow/building-llm-as-a-judge-for-ai-product-evaluation-using-critique-shadowing)
- **Juries and ensembles reduce single-judge noise and bias.** — [orq.ai juries](https://orq.ai/blog/llm-juries-in-practice); [arXiv 2609.17857](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17857)

### Inferences
- For a dataset of hundreds of items with per-dimension rubric scores and free-text rationales:
  - Compute inter-annotator agreement per dimension first. Dimensions with low human κ (for example "aesthetic appeal") cap achievable judge agreement and may need rubric rewording or should be dropped (the measurability-filtering logic).
  - Optimize per dimension. Use a separate judge prompt or checklist per dimension to reduce halo effects, and check the judge's inter-dimension correlation matrix against the human one. Higher off-diagonal correlation in the judge signals halo.
  - Use rationales in two ways: as optimizer feedback (GEPA-style reflection) and as mined checklist items (turning recurring human complaints into explicit rubric checks).
  - Use cross-validation or bootstrap confidence intervals. Gains of a few kappa points on fewer than 100 items per dimension are often noise.
  - Keep a frozen test split and a "drift" slice relabeled periodically.

### Gaps

- No retrieved source on Dawid-Skene or other latent-truth label-aggregation methods applied to LLM-judge training sets in 2024–2026.
- No retrieved Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google first-party blog posts on judge alignment methodology (searches did not surface them; fetch to many domains blocked).
- Eugene Yan's writing on LLM evaluators (for example his "evaluating LLM-evaluators" review) was not retrieved in this session.
